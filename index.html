Cardio

Bias toward Zone 2

Matched for effort, HIIT and steady-state burn similar fat — but Zone 2 (conversational pace, ~60–70% max HR) is far gentler on recovery, which matters when sleep is short. Keep HIIT to one 20-minute session a week, max.

NEAT — the silent lever

8,000–10,000 steps

Non-exercise movement can swing daily burn by up to ~2,000 kcal between similar people — and it quietly drops when you diet. When the scale stalls, add 1,000–2,000 steps before cutting food.

  • →Walking meetings & hourly desk breaks
  • →A 10-min walk after each meal
  • →Stairs, far parking, pram laps with the toddler
Recovery for tired parents

Train smart on no sleep

Keep lifting to 3 days so muscles get 48h+ to recover. On a bad-sleep day, swap HIIT for a Zone 2 walk. Deload (cut volume ~40%) every 6–8 weeks or after a rough stretch.

Rest — the fat-loss lever you're skipping

Sleep decides where
the weight comes off

In a controlled trial, dieters on identical calories who slept 5.5h vs 8.5h lost the same total weight — but far less of it was fat, and far more was muscle. With a 2-year-old you can't guarantee 8 hours. You can stack the odds.

Protect the sleep you can get

Seven realistic moves

  • 1Anchor your wake time. A fixed rise time — even after a broken night — steadies your body clock more than a fixed bedtime.
  • 2Guard an 8h window. Be in bed for the opportunity even if sleep fragments; the buffer absorbs night-wakings.
  • 3Caffeine off by 2pm. Half-life ~5–6h; even 6h before bed it measurably cuts sleep.
  • 4Nap smart. 20–30 min on weekdays; one 90-min cycle on a weekend. Avoid late-afternoon naps.
 

.

  • 5Wind-down ritual (30–60 min). Warm shower → dim lights below ~100 lux → read. The same sequence nightly conditions sleep onset.
  • 6Limit alcohol. It fragments the back half of the night and suppresses early REM.
  • 7On low-sleep days: swap HIIT for a walk, front-load protein at breakfast, lean on pre-planned meals so willpower isn't the gatekeeper. Don't catastrophise one bad night.
Short-term hormone effects vary between studies — but the body-composition and behavioural effects are robust. Either way: protect sleep.

How to read progress

Trends, not daily noise

  • →Weigh daily, judge the weekly average. Day-to-day swings of 1–2 kg are water, salt, carbs and poor sleep — not fat.
  • →Track protein & calories while you learn portions, then loosen to "protein + portion awareness".
  • →Monthly waist + photos catch recomposition the scale hides early on.
  • →Your strength log is proof: lifts holding or rising in a deficit = muscle preserved.
Eat more
Losing >1 kg/week for 2+ weeks, or lifts dropping → nudge calories up. Lean mass is at risk.
Then trim
Genuinely adherent but stalled 3 weeks → add 1,000–2,000 steps first, then cut ~100–150 kcal.
Hold & fix
Two bad-sleep weeks → cut HIIT, hold calories steady, repair sleep before pushing the deficit harder.
Diet breaks

Plan the pauses

Every 6–8 weeks, take 1–2 weeks at maintenance calories. Evidence (MATADOR) suggests scheduled breaks help retain metabolic rate and adherence with similar fat loss — and they're a sanity-saver for a stressed parent. A tool, not a cheat code.

The finish line that isn't one

Transition to maintenance

At 75 kg, recalc your numbers (BMR ≈ 1,683; TDEE ≈ 2,610) and add back ~250–500 kcal gradually over 2–4 weeks. Keep everything else: the lifts, the steps, the protein, the weekly weigh-in. The deficit ends; the habits don't.
